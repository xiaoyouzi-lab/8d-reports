# Offsite GEO Distribution Pack

## Purpose

This pack prepares high-quality offsite content for LinkedIn, Medium, Quora,
and Reddit-safe discussion. It supports the revenue evidence strategy by
answering practical manufacturing quality questions where SQEs, quality
managers, and supplier quality teams already look for help.

Do not auto-post. Do not spam. Do not fabricate personal experience. Do not use
fake statistics, fake customer logos, fake user stories, or "best in the world"
claims. Every item should be manually reviewed, platform-native, and lightly
linked only when the link genuinely helps the reader.

## Tracking

Track distribution manually in the operating sheet or weekly revenue evidence
notes:

- platform
- topic
- post or answer URL
- date posted
- role/audience
- link used yes/no
- demo download, service CTA, signup, or lead outcome if observable
- follow-up action

Do not store private respondent details, customer names, product names, problem
descriptions, root cause text, corrective action text, lessons learned, uploaded
file content, AI prompts, or raw AI output in marketing tracking.
Forbidden tracking metadata includes customer names, product names, problem descriptions, root cause text, corrective action text, lessons learned, uploaded file content, AI prompts, and raw AI output.

| 1 | How do I write an 8D report for a customer complaint? | Start with the customer risk: affected product, quantity, complaint date, and containment. The biggest mistake is jumping to action before D2/D4 are clear. | Link to `/resources/how-to-write-8d-report-customer-complaint` if the thread asks for a full structure. |
| 2 | What is the difference between SCAR and 8D? | A SCAR is the request for supplier corrective action. An 8D is a structured response format. | 4 | What should be in D4 of an 8D report? | D4 should verify why the defect occurred and why detection missed it. Separate occurrence cause from escape cause, show evidence, and rule out alternatives before choosing D5 actions. | Link to `/resources/8d-root-cause-d4-guide`. |
| 5 | What is a good corrective action in D5? | A good D5 action directly addresses a verified D4 cause, has an owner and due date, changes the process or control, and has a D6 validation plan. | 6 | How do you validate corrective actions in 8D? | D6 should show implementation and effectiveness. Include method, sample size or monitoring period, result, owner, and remaining risk. "Completed" is not validation by itself. | Link to `/resources/8d-validation-d6-guide`. |
| 7 | What are lessons learned in 8D? | Lessons learned should tell future teams what changed and what to check first if a similar issue appears. They should preserve reusable knowledge, not only thank the team. | Link to `/resources/8d-lessons-learned-d8-guide`. |
| 8 | Can AI help write an 8D report? | AI can help review gaps and weak logic, but it should not invent measurements, root causes, evidence, or customer acceptance. Treat AI as a conservative reviewer, not an approver. | Link to `/resources/ai-8d-report-checker`. |
| 9 | What should a supplier corrective action request include? | Include defect description, affected scope, containment, root cause, corrective action, validation, prevention, owner, due date, and closure evidence. | 10 | How can I customize my 8D report template? | Start by mapping your current Word, Excel, or PowerPoint form to D0-D8, required exports, evidence needs, reviewers, and one real sample issue. | 11 | What is the best 8D report software for small teams? | Look for structured D0-D8 fields, attachments, export formats, sharing, revision history, and historical reuse. | 14 | What is the most common 8D mistake? | The most common mistake is weak cause/action traceability: D4 says one thing, D5 fixes another, and D6 validates neither. Review the chain before submission. | Link to D4 or D5 guide depending on the question. |
